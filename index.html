Government of Canada migration timeline

The Cyber Centre's roadmap for the migration to post-quantum cryptography (ITSM.40.001) applies to non-classified Government of Canada systems. It sets two completion milestones: high-priority systems migrated by the end of 2031 () and the remaining systems by the end of 2035 (). Recommended algorithms are covered separately, in ITSP.40.111.

These federal milestones do not directly assess or represent the obligations of non-federal organizations. Most of the domains measured here are not federal systems. The timeline is shown as context for progress relative to the federal migration milestones.

About this monitor

This is a public monitor of how fast post-quantum cryptography (PQC) is reaching public websites. The main view focuses on Canada; the map compares Canada to other countries. Each domain is contacted with a TLS handshake; the monitor records its TLS version, the key-exchange group it negotiates, its certificate signature, and the network / CDN that serves it. A domain counts as PQC-enabled when the group it negotiates is the hybrid X25519MLKEM768.

ML-KEM is the key-encapsulation mechanism standardised by NIST in FIPS 203. X25519MLKEM768 is a TLS 1.3 group that combines classical X25519 with ML-KEM-768, specified in the IETF draft on post-quantum hybrid ECDHE-MLKEM key agreement for TLS 1.3.

The monitor measures the machine your browser actually talks to. Most domains sit behind a CDN like Cloudflare or Akamai, and it is that CDN's server, not the domain's own, that finishes the handshake. So the PQC endpoint column says whose machine did the post-quantum part: CDN edge, or no CDN detected for a domain running it on its own servers.

It does not say who decided to turn it on: the handshake cannot tell whether a domain asked its provider for post-quantum or the provider switched it on for every customer at once. It also says nothing about the origin server behind the CDN, internal services, stored data, certificate-management processes, or overall cryptographic agility.

Why it matters: an attacker can record encrypted traffic today and decrypt it once quantum computers arrive ("harvest now, decrypt later"). Hybrid post-quantum key exchange helps reduce that harvest-now-decrypt-later risk for the measured TLS connection. Post-quantum certificate signatures are a separate, slower migration. No public certificate authority issues them yet.

The dataset is re-scanned periodically; use the Scan date menu to compare snapshots over time. Built for the NSERC Summer 2026 research project by E. Deveci, supervised by Prof. Samer Lahoud, Faculty of Computer Science, Dalhousie University.
